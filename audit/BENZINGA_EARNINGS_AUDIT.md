## Recommendation: ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS

Benzinga is materially better than the timing source Breakwater has. It covers 502 of the 503 current tickers under exact spelling — all 503 once class-share spellings are mapped — gives a real clock (not an hour-rounded one) on 92.5% of 296,334 records, and where an independent source disagrees the vendor is usually the one that is right. On the 11,865 events where both sources name an unambiguous window it agrees 99.63% of the time, and the two imply the same anchor session on 99.42% of 11,865 anchorable events. Of the 12,047 comparable events, only 17 (0.14%) are genuine contradictions about which session the news preceded; the rest are the reference's own scheduling placeholders or a one-day date convention that anchors identically. Critically, it extends usable timing back to 2015, where Breakwater's present coverage is ~0% before 2020 and 25.0% overall.

It is **not** accepted unconditionally. Four conditions, each of which is a real defect found here rather than boilerplate caution:

1. **Do not join on today's ticker string.** The vendor spells class shares inconsistently and inconsistently with Breakwater: `BF-B` exists only as `BF.B` (54 records) and `BFB` (4), and `BRK-B` holds 29 records while `BRK.B` holds 62. A literal join silently loses those and reads as a coverage gap rather than a bug. A spelling map, plus the 9 universe tickers whose history stops and restarts (SNDK's symbol is reused across a 3,297-day hole under the *same* company name), must be resolved before ingestion.

2. **Reject `00:00:00`, never classify it.** 22,328 records (7.5%) carry the vendor's filler midnight. Read literally it is BMO. It must stay UNKNOWN.

3. **Read `time` as New York local wall clock, not as the documented "EST".** §2 establishes this empirically. Applying a fixed UTC-5 reading would move every EDT event an hour and reclassify a slice of them across the 16:00 cut.

4. **Paginate forward or partitioned, never `date.desc`.** The vendor's descending cursor returns 180,033 of 296,334 records and then reports itself finished, with no error — a silent 39% loss (§1.1). This is a vendor defect that any future incremental fetch could walk straight into.

Two limits are inherent rather than fixable. Usable timing does not reach a steady state until **2015** (16,325 of 18,003 records, 90.7%, against 56.1% in 2013), and ≥80% of today's universe does not carry 28 prior timed events until year end **2019**. A Phase 3 walk-forward on per-stock event statistics therefore cannot honestly claim a start date before 2019, whatever the earliest row says.

This recommendation is about the SOURCE only. Nothing was ingested, no reaction was rebuilt, no model was fitted, and nothing here says anything about whether the model is any good.

### 1.1 Is the acquisition actually complete?

A cursor API reports no total, so "followed `next_url` until it stopped" is a statement about the client. The record set was therefore re-derived two other ways and compared by `benzinga_id`.

- **Partitioned re-acquisition** — one query per calendar year, 2009–2029. Every year returned in a single response, so this traversal barely used the cursor at all. Result: **296,334 ids, identical to the snapshot's 296,334 — 0 missing, 0 extra.** That is the completeness evidence.

- **Reverse-order walk** (`date.desc,ticker.desc`) — returned **180,033 records in 4 responses and then reported no further cursor**, losing 116,301 records (39.2%) with no error of any kind. It still spanned the full date range (2028-09-01 down to 2010-04-30), so a consumer checking date coverage would see nothing wrong.

**This is a vendor defect and it is the most dangerous finding in this report.** A descending cursor walk fails silently and completely plausibly. Any future incremental fetch must paginate forward or partition by date, and must verify its own record count rather than trusting the absence of a `next_url`.

## 2. The `time` field is New York local time, not "EST"

The vendor documents `time` as *"The time (formatted as 24-hour HH:MM:SS EST) when the earnings are scheduled or were reported"*. Read literally that is a fixed UTC-5 clock, which during EDT would sit one hour behind New York local — and one hour is exactly the distance between AMC (16:00) and INTRADAY (15:00). The field was **not** reinterpreted on the strength of that word. Both readings were scored against the independent yfinance timestamps, split by whether New York was on EST or EDT at the time of the event:

| period | events | window_agree_as_published | window_agree_fixed_est | median_delta_min_as_published | median_delta_min_fixed_est | within_5min_as_published | within_5min_fixed_est | exactly_minus_60_as_published |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EST (winter) | 3786 | 0.9688 | 0.9688 | 5.0000 | 5.0000 | 0.4620 | 0.4620 | 0.0156 |
| EDT (summer) | 8261 | 0.9874 | 0.9690 | 6.0000 | 66.0000 | 0.4492 | 0.0143 | 0.0127 |
| all | 12047 | 0.9816 | 0.9690 | 5.0000 | 60.0000 | 0.4532 | 0.1550 | 0.0136 |

The two readings are identical during EST by construction, and they are: 0.9688 either way. The whole signal is in the EDT rows, where taking the value **as published** agrees with the independent source 0.9874 of the time and shifting it by +1h agrees only 0.9690 of the time. If the field were genuinely fixed EST the ordering would be reversed and the as-published reading would collapse in summer. It does not.

**Conclusion: the historical values behave as New York LOCAL market wall time (DST-aware).** They are therefore consumed as naive America/New_York wall clock — the same convention `utilities/time_utilities.py` defines for `announce_ts_ny` — with no DST shift applied anywhere. The vendor's "EST" is a loose synonym for Eastern Time.

## 7. What this work deliberately did not do

- Nothing was ingested into `db/breakwater.duckdb`. This package contains no database code at all, and `testing/test_massive_earnings.py` asserts statically that it never acquires any.
- No reaction, anchor or target was rebuilt. No model was fitted or evaluated. No threshold, weight or scoring function was touched.
- No point-in-time index membership was reconstructed.
- Raw and normalized vendor data are gitignored: licensed third-party data in a public repository, and a raw vendor file must never become a production input by accident.
